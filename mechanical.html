Mechanical — MEP notes

Plumbing, drainage, HVAC: how to read the drawings and what the abbreviations mean. Started 19-9-2026 from the villa drainage plans · ← hub

In one paragraph

A drainage sheet carries three separate pipe systems: soil (WC waste, red), waste (basins, showers, kitchens, yellow) and storm (rain, black). Each run goes to ground through a manhole (MH); where surface water has to be picked up from the paving, a catch basin (CB) sits next to the manhole and drains into it. Inside the house the small circles are floor drains (FD); a WCO is the clean-out plug you open to rod a blocked pipe.

1 · Drainage drawing legend

As used on the villa ground-floor drainage plan (ø110 / ø160 SP/UG, MH + CB pairs on the two vertical runs)
Abbrev.MeaningOn the drawing / what it does
CBCatch basinSquare box with a hatched grate, drawn next to a manhole. Surface inlet: collects rain or wash-down water from the paving through the grate, holds silt in a sump at the bottom, discharges into the adjacent MH.
MHManholeSquare chamber with a round cover. Access point on the sewer or storm line: pipes change direction, size or level here; where you rod the line.
FDFloor drainSmall square with a circle, in baths, maid room, boiler and pool technical rooms. Trapped gully in the floor, usually ø50–ø75 to the nearest waste line.
WCOWall clean-outAccess plug in the pipe, at a wall, to push a rod or a jet through a blocked run. Placed at the top of a run and at changes of direction.
COClean-outSame, when it sits in the floor or on the pipe itself.
SP/UGSoil pipe, undergroundRed line. Carries WC discharge (black water). ø110 for branches, ø160 for the collector.
WP/UGWaste pipe, undergroundYellow line. Grey water: basins, showers, kitchen, floor drains. Usually ø75–ø110.
RWPRain-water pipeBlack line / black arrows. Storm water from roofs and terraces, down to the storm manholes.
VPVent pipeOpen stack to the roof that lets air into the soil system so traps keep their water seal.
ø110, ø160Pipe nominal diameterOutside diameter in mm (uPVC / PP). ø110 ≈ 4", ø160 ≈ 6", ø75 ≈ 3", ø50 ≈ 2".
4"Four-inch pipeImperial size on the same sheet (boiler / pool room lines) — same as ø110.
TFTop of finish (level)Finished-floor level in metres, e.g. +458.55. Invert levels are read against it.
ILInvert levelLevel of the inside bottom of the pipe at a manhole — what sets the slope.
Arrow ▼Flow directionFilled arrow on the line = direction of fall. Text "SLOPE 1%" or "2%" beside it = gradient.
Rule of thumb for slopes: ø110 soil at 1–2 %, ø160 collector at 0.7–1 %, waste ø75 at 2 %. Every change of direction underground gets a manhole or a clean-out.

3 · Every water item in the villa — real photo + drawing symbol

Added 21-9-2026 from the water-tank study (raw 20 m³ / treated 6 m³ in the 23.7 m² basement room + 2 000 L roof tank; rain from the 80 m² roof and the hard terraces). Left = what it looks like on site, right = how it appears on the AutoCAD drainage plan (cropped from the plan where it exists, drawn in plan style where it is still to be added).

A · Rain-water collection

Roof rain-water outlet + leaf screen RWO / rain head
Green circle on the roof/terrace edge = ø110 (4") rain-water outlet, one per roof zone. Fit a domed leaf screen on it and a rain head on the downpipe so leaves never reach the tank.
→ rain tank
First-flush diverter FF
Vertical dead chamber on the downpipe: the first 20–30 L of every storm (dust, bird droppings) fill it and drip out through the small tap; the clean water then passes to the tank. Not on the current plan — to add on each roof downpipe.
→ rain tank / drain
Trench (channel) drain 20×20 TRENCH DRAIN
Linear grate flush with the terrace paving; the terrace slopes to it. On the plan: the orange hatched bar labelled TRENCH DRAIN 20×20 cm beside the pump/treatment room.
→ silt trap → rain tank (winter) / storm (summer)
Catch basin CB
Square box with a grate and a removable silt basket, outlet pipe at the bottom. On the plan: the orange hatched square drawn next to every MH. This is the trap the terrace water passes before the rain tank.
→ adjacent MH / rain tank
Manhole MH
Square chamber with a round cover — the circle-in-square on the plan (left of each CB). Access point where pipes change direction, size or level; where the line is rodded.
→ sewer or storm line
Floor drain FD
Trapped stainless gully in bathrooms, laundry, boiler and pump rooms. Small orange square with a circle on the plan ("FD").
→ waste pipe ø50–75
Wall clean-out WCO
Screw plug on the soil pipe at a wall, top of each run. Red circle with a diagonal on the plan. Opened to push a rod or jet through a blockage.
→ —
Seasonal diverter valve 3-WAY
L-port 3-way ball valve on the terrace-drain line: to the rain tank from the first November rains, to the storm line May–October (dust, pool chemicals, terrace washing). Not on the plan — to add in the pump room.
→ rain tank ⇄ storm
Planter drain PD
Bottom outlet of every built planter: waterproofing → drainage cell / gravel → geotextile → soil. Water that percolated through soil, with fertiliser — never into the tank. Give it an inspection box; it is the first thing that clogs.
→ storm only
Perforated pipe (French drain) PERF ø110
Slotted pipe in a gravel trench wrapped in geotextile, 0.5–1 % slope, at the foot of every retaining wall and around the B2 perimeter at footing level. Collects groundwater, not rain — protects the tank-room waterproofing.
→ storm / soakaway
Rain-water tank (underground, concrete) RWT
Receives roof + clean terrace water. 10–12 m³ is enough (125 m³/yr arrives in bursts). Float-controlled transfer into the raw tank; overflow to the storm line; manhole 60×60, vent, potable-grade coating.
→ raw tank by float

B · Storage & supply

Raw tank 20 m³ + treated tank 6 m³ RAW / TREATED
Two concrete compartments in the 23.7 m² basement room (2.3×4.3 m and 1.5×2.0 m, 2.0 m water). Raw = state + tanker + rain, feeds treatment, garden and pool. Treated = after filters, feeds the roof tank. Floors slope to a sump, flush manholes as in the photo.
→ treatment / roof tank
Float valve FV
Brass float valve on every tank inlet (state water into raw, transfer into treated, roof tank). Closes mechanically when the tank is full — no electricity, no overflow.
→ —
Tanker fill point 3" CAMLOCK
3" aluminium camlock coupling with dust cap in a small lockable box at road level, piped straight into the raw tank only. Tanker connects in 10 seconds; nothing enters the treated side.
→ raw tank
Transfer pump TP
Self-priming jet pump (Pedrollo JSW class, ~1 HP) that moves raw water through the filters into the treated tank, and treated water up to the roof tank on the roof float's call. Second small pump for garden / pool make-up from raw.
→ filters → treated → roof
Roof tank 2000 L RT
Polyethylene, opaque, insulated or shaded, lockable lid, overflow to the rain downpipe. Daily buffer (~1.5 days) filled by float from the treated tank; the house is fed from it via the booster + UV, so it turns over every day.
→ booster → UV → house

C · Treatment train (in the plant area of the tank room)

Auto-backwash multimedia filter MMF
Fibreglass tank of sand/anthracite with a Runxin/Fleck control valve on top (photo = the valve head). Removes silt and turbidity; backwashes itself to drain on a timer, so no cartridges to change.
→ → carbon
Activated-carbon filter GAC
Big Blue 20" housing with a GAC cartridge (basic tier) or a second backwash tank of carbon (recommended). Takes out chlorine, taste and odour from state and tanker water.
→ → softener / treated tank
Water softener SOFT
Resin tank + brine (salt) tank. Only if the lab test shows hardness above ~250 mg/L CaCO₃ — likely for Ajaltoun limestone water. ~10 kg salt/month. Protects boilers, taps and the UV sleeve from scale.
→ → treated tank
UV disinfection UV
Stainless chamber with a UV-C lamp, 40–60 L/min, 40 mJ/cm². Placed LAST, on the down-feed from the roof tank just before the house, so water is disinfected at the moment of use, not before storage. Lamp + quartz sleeve yearly.
→ → house
Variable-speed booster VSD BOOSTER
Grundfos Scala2 class: keeps constant pressure (3 bar) at every tap regardless of floor; quiet, self-priming, runs on the villa's solar/battery circuit. Feeds the house from the roof tank line through the UV.
→ → UV → house
Kitchen reverse osmosis RO
5-stage under-sink unit with its own small tank and tap: drinking and cooking water only. This replaces any whole-house RO idea — far cheaper and no water wasted on showers and garden.
→ kitchen tap
Rule to remember: a grate on top = surface water you can keep (roof, clean paving) → rain tank; a pipe under soil or in the ground (planter drain, perforated pipe) = dirty water → storm. Photos are reference pictures of the item type, not the villa.
