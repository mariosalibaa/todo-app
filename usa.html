Youssef · dug well, 40 ft · Zoeller ½ HP 115 V · switch Square D 40/60

Fixing the well pump

The pump was packed in sand, so it sucks sand, jams, hums on start-up and trips. The fixes below keep the sand out, keep the motor cool, and stop it from running dry.

Order of work

  1. Back to the 15 A breaker (20 A is not safe for the wire).
  2. New pressure switch Square D 40/60 (FSG2J24, same as the old one) + ½" cable clamp; don’t touch the adjusting nuts. Tank air 38 psi with the pump off and the water drained.
  3. New filter cartridge.
  4. Still hums and trips at start-up? Power OFF, someone at the top, pull the pump. Turn the shaft by hand: stiff or gritty = replace with the same model.
  5. Reinstall it out of the sand, using one of the set-ups below (5 is the simplest).
  6. Add the Pumptec 5800020610 and a spin-down sand separator.

1Flow sleeve (cooling shroud)

A short piece of pipe, closed at the top around the drop pipe and open at the bottom, slipped over the pump. Water has to enter from below and pass along the motor before it reaches the intake, which cools the motor. It also stops sand falling from above straight into the intake. Needed whenever the pump lies on its side or sits in moving water.

How to make it:4" PVC drain pipe, about 30 cm longer than the pump, cap the top with a hole for the drop pipe, seal with a hose clamp + tape. Cost: under $30 at Home Depot.

2Sump in gravel (keep the pump out of the sand)

At the bottom of the dug well, stand a slotted pipe or a perforated barrel in clean gravel and hang the pump inside it, above the bottom. Water seeps in through the gravel and slots; the sand stays outside.

3Dry-run protection (Franklin Pumptec)

A box next to the pressure switch that watches the pump’s current. When the water runs low and the pump starts running dry, the current drops, and it cuts power, then retries after a set time. It also cuts power on bad voltage. It needs no wires down the well.

Buy:Franklin Pumptec 5800020610 (115/230 V). About $200. An electrician wires it between the pressure switch and the pump, then calibrates it once with the pump running normally.

4Sand separator (spin-down filter)

A clear housing with a washable mesh screen, fitted before the cartridge filter. Sand spins down and collects at the bottom; open the red valve to flush it out. The cartridge filter stops clogging.

Buy:1" spin-down filter, 60 or 100 mesh (60 for coarse sand). $40–80. Install between the pressure tank and the existing cartridge filter; flush it weekly at first.

5Float it inside a mesh pipe

From an RPS Solar Pumps video on pumping from shallow, sandy springs. The pump goes inside a drilled PVC pipe wrapped in fine mesh, capped at one end with a well seal at the other. The pipe is tied under floats so it sits just below the water surface, away from the sand at the bottom, and follows the water level. In shallow water it floats on its side.

For this pump:use a 6" pipe (the Zoeller is 4" wide and won’t fit a 4" pipe). Use more floats than in the video, because this pump is heavier. Tie it to a rope or stainless cable so the power cable never takes the weight. Keep the intake below the lowest water level, and let the Pumptec stop it when the water runs out.
