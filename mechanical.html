Plumbing, drainage, HVAC: how to read the drawings and what the abbreviations mean. Started 19-9-2026 from the villa drainage plans · ← hub

In one paragraph

A drainage sheet carries three separate pipe systems: soil (WC waste, red), waste (basins, showers, kitchens, yellow) and storm (rain, black). Each run goes to ground through a manhole (MH); where surface water has to be picked up from the paving, a catch basin (CB) sits next to the manhole and drains into it. Inside the house the small circles are floor drains (FD); a WCO is the clean-out plug you open to rod a blocked pipe.

1 · Drainage drawing legend

As used on the villa ground-floor drainage plan (ø110 / ø160 SP/UG, MH + CB pairs on the two vertical runs)
Abbrev.MeaningOn the drawing / what it does
CBCatch basinSquare box with a hatched grate, drawn next to a manhole. Surface inlet: collects rain or wash-down water from the paving through the grate, holds silt in a sump at the bottom, discharges into the adjacent MH.
MHManholeSquare chamber with a round cover. Access point on the sewer or storm line: pipes change direction, size or level here; where you rod the line.
FDFloor drainSmall square with a circle, in baths, maid room, boiler and pool technical rooms. Trapped gully in the floor, usually ø50–ø75 to the nearest waste line.
WCOWall clean-outAccess plug in the pipe, at a wall, to push a rod or a jet through a blocked run. Placed at the top of a run and at changes of direction.
COClean-outSame, when it sits in the floor or on the pipe itself.
SP/UGSoil pipe, undergroundRed line. Carries WC discharge (black water). ø110 for branches, ø160 for the collector.
WP/UGWaste pipe, undergroundYellow line. Grey water: basins, showers, kitchen, floor drains. Usually ø75–ø110.
RWPRain-water pipeBlack line / black arrows. Storm water from roofs and terraces, down to the storm manholes.
VPVent pipeOpen stack to the roof that lets air into the soil system so traps keep their water seal.
ø110, ø160Pipe nominal diameterOutside diameter in mm (uPVC / PP). ø110 ≈ 4", ø160 ≈ 6", ø75 ≈ 3", ø50 ≈ 2".
4"Four-inch pipeImperial size on the same sheet (boiler / pool room lines) — same as ø110.
TFTop of finish (level)Finished-floor level in metres, e.g. +458.55. Invert levels are read against it.
ILInvert levelLevel of the inside bottom of the pipe at a manhole — what sets the slope.
Arrow ▼Flow directionFilled arrow on the line = direction of fall. Text "SLOPE 1%" or "2%" beside it = gradient.
Rule of thumb for slopes: ø110 soil at 1–2 %, ø160 collector at 0.7–1 %, waste ø75 at 2 %. Every change of direction underground gets a manhole or a clean-out.
